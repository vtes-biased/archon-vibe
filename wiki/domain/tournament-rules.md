# VEKN tournament rules

The rules the app implements. Source throughout: `reference/tournament-rules.md`,
section numbers cited inline; © Black Chantry Productions. Where the app
deliberately or accidentally diverges, the divergence is recorded in
[implementation](../tournaments.md), not here — this page states the rule.

## Eligibility and materials

Any player may enter an open, non-invitational sanctioned tournament **except**
the tournament organizer of record, the head judge and any other listed judges of
record, players currently suspended by VEKN, and players prohibited by VEKN or
Black Chantry policy (§1.1). The organizer and judges may play only in an event
using the Multi-Judge System (§2.9) — those are the only events in which they may
participate.

Players must bring a way to track game information, and **a valid, unique VEKN
number registered in their own name**. New players register at their first
tournament; a player may hold only one VEKN number, and organizers must report
anyone using more than one (§1.2).

Wagering on any part of a tournament is forbidden for players and officials alike
(§1.3).

## Roles

The **tournament organizer** of record is ultimately responsible for all
operations and reporting: site, materials, staffing including finding a head
judge, advertising, retaining results for one full year, and reporting results to
VEKN in a timely manner. These may be delegated (§2.2).

The **head judge** must be physically present during play, is the final judicial
authority, may appoint other judges with the organizer's agreement, and is
responsible for reporting all warnings to VEKN directly or through the organizer's
event report (§2.6). A judge's decision may be appealed to the head judge, whose
decision is final; players may not appeal before the responding judge has ruled
(§2.7). Organizer and head judge may be the same person (§2.6). A judge who takes
more than a minute over a ruling may extend the game time appropriately; the
extension must be clearly communicated and recorded immediately (§2.8).

The **scorekeeper** receives and records game results, constructs seatings,
ensures accurate result entry and removes players from the event; the head judge
or organizer may also be the scorekeeper (Appendix B).

A **spectator** is anyone other than a judge or organizer who is not playing in
that game — including a player ousted from it, for the remainder of the round.
Spectators stay silent and must not communicate with players (§2.4).

**Multi-Judge System** (§2.9): usable at any non-qualifier, non-championship
tournament with at least eight participants. The organizer announces it before the
tournament and identifies six judges in rank order. The head judge makes all
rulings except in a game where they were seated, in which case the next-ranked
unseated judge rules.

## Round structure

A tournament is **a minimum of three rounds** (§3.1). After two or more
preliminary rounds, players are ranked by Game Wins, tie-broken by total Victory
Points from the preliminary rounds, then by Tournament Points. **Remaining ties for
any of the top five rankings are resolved by a fair random method.** The five
highest-ranked players advance to the final round (§3.1).

**Time limit**: a minimum of two hours per round, announced before play. The final
round may run longer at the judge's option. When the round ends the judge notifies
all players, and the game ends with the current minion action, or at the end of
the current phase if the notification did not fall in the minion phase (§3.1.1).

### Preliminary seating (§3.1.2)

Players are grouped **randomly and impartially**, without regard to identity or
personal relationships. Groups of five, and where the count is not divisible by
five, groups of four and five such that **as many groups as possible contain
five**.

Where players cannot be divided into groups of 4 and 5 — **6, 7 or 11 players** —
the judge may use an arrangement in which some players sit out each round, while
still ensuring every player plays the same number of games, usually one less than
the number of rounds.

Each table has five positions numbered clockwise; the player in position one plays
first. At four-player tables the empty position is ignored for turn rotation.

**The judge must ensure exact predator-prey relationships are not duplicated from
round to round whenever possible**, rearranging the affected table randomly and
impartially when they are. The judge may also, at their discretion, reseat players
who shared a table in a previous round.

The rules text names no further optimization criteria. It notes only that use of
"the optimal seating chart, found in The Archon tournament tracking spreadsheet,
is encouraged" — so any richer priority list is a convention inherited from that
spreadsheet, not a rule.

### Final-round seating (§3.1.3)

Positions are **not** assigned. Each finalist gets an index card with their name.
Each shuffles their crypt and presents it to the judge for further shuffling and
cutting; the judge draws and reveals three random cards from each crypt, and those
cards, the number of cards left in each crypt, and their ownership are public
information until everyone is seated.

Starting with the **lowest qualifier**, each player places their card faceup in a
row, choosing either end of the row (the ends are equivalent) or creating a space
between two already-placed cards. The finished row read left to right gives the
seating. The judge then randomly determines who plays first, and the revealed
crypt cards are shuffled back.

### Joining and leaving

**Late arrivals** (§3.3.1) — players arriving after the first round has started
may, at the judge's discretion, be added and play from the start of the next
round. **Players may not be added to games already in progress.**

**Tardiness** (§3.3) — a player not in their seat within 15 minutes of the
scheduled round start receives a "Loss" for that round. This does not apply to
late arrivals who were not assigned a seat.

**Bowing out** (§3.1.4) — a player leaving the event must tell the scorekeeper
before seatings for the next round are generated. Leaving after seatings have
begun means a "Loss" in the upcoming round and removal after it. Early departure
mid-preliminaries is handled the same way at the judge's discretion (§3.3.2).

**In-game breaks** (§3.3.3) require notifying a judge first, so the table's time
limit can be extended.

### Multi-deck system (§3.1.5)

Deck modification between rounds is forbidden **unless** the tournament uses the
multi-deck system, which may be used at any tournament that is **neither a
national championship, a Grand Prix, nor a continental championship**, and must be
announced in advance. Players may bring any number of legal decks and swap decks
or cards freely between rounds.

For the finals the organizer must announce, before round 1, which method applies
(§3.1.5a):

- **Best-Performing Deck** — the finalist plays the deck with the highest single
  preliminary-round score, ranked GW, then VP, then TP; ties broken randomly among
  the tied decks.
- **Free Choice** — the finalist plays any deck they used in the preliminaries.

### Tournament without a final (§3.1.6)

**In a tournament with fewer than 8 players** — fewer than two tables — the
organizer may omit the final round, or run an additional preliminary round in its
place. The decision must be announced before the first round. Final standings then
come from the preliminary rounds by the §3.1 ranking. It combines with or without
the multi-deck system at the organizer's option. **A tournament begun under the
no-final arrangement cannot admit more players between rounds if that would create
a round with 8 or more players.**

## Scoring

Three point types (§3.7): **Game Wins** to the winner of each game, **Victory
Points** for ousting a prey and for surviving, **Tournament Points** by table
ranking.

**Game Win** (§3.7.1) — awarded at the end of a game to a player with **at least
2 Victory Points and more Victory Points than any other player** at that table. No
game win is awarded on a tie.

**Victory Points** (§3.7.2) — one per prey ousted; **half** a victory point for
withdrawing, rather than the full point the game rules award; half a victory point
for not being ousted when the time limit is reached, **unless** that player is the
last surviving at the table, in which case a full point as normal. Players are
then ranked first through fifth at their table by total victory points.

**Tournament Points** (§3.7.3) — five-player table: 60, 48, 36, 24, 12. Four-player
table: players are ranked first, second, fourth and fifth, third place being taken
by the empty **table bye** position. Tournament points for tied table rankings are
**averaged**. Worked examples in §3.7.4.

**Final round** (§3.7.5) — the player with the highest victory-point total **from
the final round only** is the tournament winner. A tie is broken by preliminary
standings. **The rest of the finalists tie for second, with no further tiebreak
attempted.**

**Conceding** (§3.5) — permitted at any time provided all but one player agree and
it does not violate play-to-win. The result is recorded as if the remaining player
had ousted the conceding players in sequence.

**Intentional draw** (§3.6) — players may mutually agree at any time before results
are submitted; the result is the same as playing to the time limit. A declined
offer must not be pressed further.

## Formats

**Standard Constructed** is the only constructed format VEKN sanctions (§6.1).
Decks are 60–90 library cards with a crypt of at least 12 (§6.2). The organizer may
require deck registration on arrival, after which the deck may not be altered
(§6.3).

Sanctioned tournaments run **with card set restrictions do not count toward VEKN
player ratings** (§6.1.1).

**V5** (§6.4) is Standard Constructed over a reduced card pool — the Fifth Edition
preconstructed decks, New Blood packs, and a named list of other releases, with new
releases added on their release date.

**Limited** (§7) covers Sealed and Draft formats: Standard Sealed (§7.3),
Restricted Trade (§7.4), Booster Draft (§7.6), Table/Rochester Draft (§7.7), Mixed
or Elder Draft (§7.8), plus the alternative constructed **Restricted format**
(§7.9), where the organizer sets deck-content restrictions that must be shown on
the tournament calendar.

Limited deck minimums scale with boosters per player (§7.2.1): with 8+ boosters
the library minimum is 40, otherwise 5 cards per booster with **recursions** to
bring the effective library size near 60 (§7.2.2); the library never exceeds 90;
the crypt minimum is 12 with 12+ boosters, otherwise the booster count. **Limited
crypts may contain cards from any groups with no group-number limitation.**

A player who has received sealed product may not leave before the first round ends;
doing so means returning the product, a "Loss" for the round and being dropped
(§7.2.6).

## Cards

All cards must be genuine VTES or Jyhad cards, or official VEKN-issued cards from
vekn.net (§4.1). §4.1.1 holds the **banned list**, banned in all VEKN tournaments
in both constructed and limited.

**New releases** are legal in Constructed play 30 days after retail release, and
in Limited play immediately, including before release at a prerelease (§4.3).

**Proxy cards** (§4.5) — the organizer of an event that is **not** a National
Championship, Grand Prix or Continental Championship may allow them. There is no
limit on the number; each must be a colour copy on standard paper legible enough
to read the illustrator's name; the organizer must mark the event as allowing
proxy cards **on the VEKN calendar**. Otherwise proxies are permitted only when a
judge provides one for a damaged or worn card.

Cards are interpreted by their most recent printing, the head judge being the
final authority (§4.2).

## Conduct

**Play to win** (§4.8) — players must not pursue goals conflicting with the game's
own; in a tournament that means playing for a Game Win where reasonably possible,
and otherwise for as many Victory Points as possible. Deals are neither enforced
nor regulated, but a deal that was in the players' best interests when made may be
honored even where play-to-win would otherwise require breaking it. **Exception:**
when only two Methuselahs remain, prior deals are void and both must play to win
on game state alone. In the finals, playing to win means playing to finish as
tournament winner.

**Cheating** (§5.1) — the head judge reviews all allegations and applies the
Judge's Guide. All disqualifications are subject to later VEKN review. Cheating
includes outside assistance, looking at card faces while shuffling, collusion,
misrepresenting rules or errata, marked cards, drawing extra cards, manipulating
draws, stalling, and misrepresenting or obscuring public information.

**Unsportsmanlike conduct** (§5.2), **slow play** (§5.3) and **marked cards**
(§5.4) each route to the Judge's Guide — see [judging](judging.md).

## Sanctioning and reporting

Organizers must apply for sanctioning **at least 28 days before the event**, and
should advertise it at least 28 days ahead including time and place (§8.1, §8.2).

Event reports are **due within 8 days** of the tournament's conclusion. Reports not
received within 8 days are listed "Not Received" for 14 days; those not received
within 15–30 days are listed **Delinquent**, and may still be submitted without
penalty; reports not received within **30 days** may be invalidated (§9.2–9.4).
An organizer showing Delinquent or Invalid two months running is investigated
(§9.5).

Players' records at **invalid** events do not count toward ratings and rankings,
and VEKN may invalidate any event's reported results, usually for fraudulent or
incorrect reporting (§9.4).

**All participants must hold a VEKN membership number before playing. Results
reported with temporary numbers, player names or placeholders are not included in
the ratings** (§9.6).

Organizers must keep copies of all reports for a year (§9.1).
